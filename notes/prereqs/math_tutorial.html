01

The Local Approximation

Why a curved landscape can look flat to a population

Core concept

A population does not experience its entire fitness landscape. It samples only the region defined by its additive genetic covariance — the covariance cloud.

Within that cloud, the fitness surface can be expanded to second order around the population mean $\bar{z}$:

$$\ell(z) \approx \ell(\bar{z}) + \underbrace{b\,\Delta z}_{L} + \underbrace{\tfrac{1}{2}H\,(\Delta z)^2}_{Q}$$

The gradient $b = d\ell/dz|_{\bar z}$ is the slope of the fitness surface at the mean. The Hessian $H = d^2\ell/dz^2|_{\bar z}$ is the curvature — how quickly the surface bends away from the tangent.

When the cloud is narrow ($\sigma$ small), the linear part $L$ dominates and the tangent line is a good predictor of fitness. When the cloud is wide, the quadratic part $Q$ becomes important and the tangent fails.

Key insight: Additive prediction works not because landscapes are simple — but because populations only see a small, locally flat piece of them.

02

The Variance Decomposition

Why $V_{\rm lin}$ scales as $G$ and $V_{\rm quad}$ scales as $G^2$

The decomposition

Under Gaussian breeding-value distributions, the cross-term $\text{Cov}(L,Q)$ vanishes by parity: $L$ is odd in $\Delta z$ but $Q$ is even, so their product integrates to zero over a symmetric distribution.

This means total log-fitness variance splits cleanly:

$$\text{Var}[\delta\ell] = V_{\rm lin} + V_{\rm quad}$$
$$V_{\rm lin} = b^\top \mathbf{G}\, b \quad\text{(one copy of } \mathbf{G}\text{)}$$
$$V_{\rm quad} = \tfrac{1}{2}\,\text{tr}[(\mathbf{H}\mathbf{G})^2] \quad\text{(two copies of } \mathbf{G}\text{)}$$

In one dimension these simplify to $V_{\rm lin} = b^2\sigma^2$ and $V_{\rm quad} = \tfrac{1}{2}H^2\sigma^4$. The additivity index is their ratio:

$$\mathcal{A}_g = \frac{V_{\rm lin}}{V_{\rm lin} + V_{\rm quad}}$$
The key structural fact: Under uniform scaling $\mathbf{G} \to s\mathbf{G}$:
$V_{\rm lin}(s) = s\,V_{\rm lin}$  ·  $V_{\rm quad}(s) = s^2 V_{\rm quad}$
So $\mathcal{A}_g(s) = \dfrac{1}{1 + s\kappa}$ — a rectangular hyperbola that always declines with $s$. Narrower clouds are always more locally additive.

03

$\mathcal{A}_g$ as a Local $R^2$

The additivity index is literally the coefficient of determination of the best linear predictor

The R² connection

Under Gaussian closure, $\mathcal{A}_g$ has a direct statistical interpretation:

$$\mathcal{A}_g = \text{Corr}(L,\, \delta\ell)^2$$

This follows because $\delta\ell = L + Q$ and $\text{Cov}(L,Q) = 0$, so:

$$\mathcal{A}_g = \frac{\text{Var}[L]}{\text{Var}[L] + \text{Var}[Q]} = R^2$$

The scatter plot shows 500 breeding values sampled from $\mathcal{N}(0, \sigma^2)$. Each point is plotted as:

$x$-axis: linear predictor $L = b\,\Delta z$
$y$-axis: true log-fitness deviation $\delta\ell = L + Q$

When $\mathcal{A}_g$ is high, points hug the identity line (perfect linear prediction). When $\mathcal{A}_g$ is low, the quadratic term scatters points away from the line.

Connecting to breeding: $\mathcal{A}_g$ is the proportion of heritable fitness variance that a genomic model using only additive effects can capture — at the current position, with the current variance structure.
Important: Heritability $h^2$ measures how much phenotypic variance is genetic. $\mathcal{A}_g$ measures, of that genetic variance, how much is captured by the linear predictor. A highly heritable trait at a fitness peak can have $\mathcal{A}_g \approx 0$.

The Moving Optimum: Three Acts

Channel entry, exit, and re-entry driven by optimum motion and selection

The one-trait model

The simplest case where all quantities can be written in closed form. Fitness is a Gaussian well moving at rate $\dot\theta$:

$$\ell(z,t) = \ell_0 - \tfrac{1}{2}\,s\,(z - \theta(t))^2$$

At the population mean: $b = s\,d$ and $H = -s$, where $d = \theta - \bar{z}$ is the lag.

$$\mathcal{A}_g = \frac{d^2}{d^2 + G/2}$$

This is the central result: the channel is open when $d^2 \gg G/2$ (the lag is large relative to variance) and closes as $d \to 0$.

Act II: The channel closes not because biology becomes complex, but because selection succeeded — the directional signal collapsed.
State equations
$$\dot{d} = \dot\theta - G\,s\,d$$
$$\dot{G} = -sG^2 - \lambda_{\rm rec}(G - G_*) + m - \frac{G}{2N_e}$$

The Rate Diagnostic $r(t)$

A signed budget of channel-opening and channel-closing forces

The logit identity

Define the logit of $\mathcal{A}_g$:

$$\mathcal{L}(t) = \ln\!\left(\frac{V_{\rm lin}}{V_{\rm quad}}\right) = \ln\!\left(\frac{\mathcal{A}_g}{1-\mathcal{A}_g}\right)$$

Its time derivative $r(t) = \dot{\mathcal{L}}$ satisfies the exact algebraic identity:

$$\dot{\mathcal{A}}_g = \mathcal{A}_g\,(1-\mathcal{A}_g)\cdot r(t)$$

This is not a model — it is a mathematical identity. $r(t)$ is the instantaneous relative advantage of linear over quadratic variance.

Five-way decomposition (1D model)
$$r = \underbrace{\frac{2\dot\theta}{d}}_{r_{\rm motion}} \underbrace{-\,sG}_{r_{\rm curv}} \underbrace{+\,\lambda\!\left(1-\frac{G_*}{G}\right)}_{r_{\rm rec}} \underbrace{-\,\frac{m}{G}}_{r_{\rm mut}} \underbrace{+\,\frac{1}{2N_e}}_{r_{\rm drift}}$$
