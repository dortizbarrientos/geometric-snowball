APP 04 / 05 · TWO-TRAIT GRADIENT ROTATION

The Silent Exit:
Gradient Rotation

"How can the channel close without any loss of genetic variance — or any change in curvature?"

Prerequisite: Apps 1–2 Key formula: $V_{\rm lin}(\theta) = \|\mathbf{b}\|^2(\lambda_1\cos^2\!\theta + \lambda_2\sin^2\!\theta)$ Notebook: Section 6
01

A silent failure mode

Apps 1–3 showed how $\mathcal{A}_g$ falls when genetic variance is broad or when the population nears its optimum. Both those routes change $V_{\rm lin}$ and $V_{\rm quad}$ together. But there is a third route — one that erodes the channel with no change in $\|\mathbf{b}\|$, no change in variance magnitude, and no change in curvature magnitude.

When the fitness gradient rotates away from the main axis of the covariance ellipse, $V_{\rm lin}$ falls while $V_{\rm quad}$ stays constant. The channel exits silently.

Think of a covariance ellipse as a stretched oval — long in one direction ($\mathbf{g}_{\max}$), short in the perpendicular direction. Selection works best when it pulls along the long axis — because that is where the most genetic variation lies.

Now imagine the environment shifts and selection begins pulling at an angle to the oval. The same total pull, but now pointed partly across the narrow direction. Less variation is available in the direction selection needs. The channel closes — not because variance changed, not because selection weakened, but because they are now misaligned.

$V_{\rm lin} = \mathbf{b}^\top\mathbf{G}\mathbf{b}$ depends on the projection of $\mathbf{b}$ onto the eigenvectors of $\mathbf{G}$. When $\mathbf{b}$ is aligned with $\mathbf{g}_{\max}$ (the leading eigenvector), $V_{\rm lin}$ is maximal. When $\mathbf{b}$ rotates toward the minor axis, $V_{\rm lin}$ falls. $V_{\rm quad} = \tfrac{1}{2}\text{tr}[(\mathbf{H}\mathbf{G})^2]$ does not involve $\mathbf{b}$ at all — it depends only on the eigenvalues of $\mathbf{H}$ and $\mathbf{G}$. So rotation reduces $V_{\rm lin}$ while leaving $V_{\rm quad}$ untouched.

This is the signature of climate-driven channel exit. El Niño years on Daphne Major rotated the seed community toward small, soft seeds — pulling the selection gradient for beak depth and beak width in a new direction. The beak covariance structure did not change immediately. But $\mathbf{b}$ rotated away from $\mathbf{g}_{\max}$, and $\mathcal{A}_g$ fell. No variance was lost. No curvature increased. The channel closed from misalignment alone.

02

Two-trait setup

Consider two traits with covariance matrix $\mathbf{G} = \text{diag}(\lambda_1, \lambda_2)$ where $\lambda_1 > \lambda_2$ (the major axis has more variance). The fitness gradient $\mathbf{b}$ makes angle $\theta$ with the major axis $\mathbf{g}_{\max}$. The Hessian is isotropic: $\mathbf{H} = -\gamma\,\mathbf{I}$.

GRADIENT ROTATION SETUP · Paper Results §4
$$\mathbf{b}(\theta) = \|\mathbf{b}\|\begin{pmatrix}\cos\theta\\\sin\theta\end{pmatrix}, \quad \mathbf{G} = \begin{pmatrix}\lambda_1 & 0 \\ 0 & \lambda_2\end{pmatrix}, \quad \mathbf{H} = -\gamma\mathbf{I}$$
$\theta=0°$ means selection is perfectly aligned with the major genetic axis. $\theta=90°$ means selection pulls entirely along the minor axis — maximum misalignment.
03

How $\mathcal{A}_g$ depends on rotation

GRADIENT-ALIGNED VARIANCE UNDER ROTATION · Paper Eq. 8
$$V_{\rm lin}(\theta) = \|\mathbf{b}\|^2\!\left(\lambda_1\cos^2\!\theta + \lambda_2\sin^2\!\theta\right)$$ $$V_{\rm quad} = \tfrac{1}{2}\gamma^2\!\left(\lambda_1^2 + \lambda_2^2\right) \quad \text{(constant in }\theta\text{)}$$
$V_{\rm lin}$ interpolates from $\|\mathbf{b}\|^2\lambda_1$ (full alignment, $\theta=0$) to $\|\mathbf{b}\|^2\lambda_2$ (full misalignment, $\theta=90°$). $V_{\rm quad}$ is independent of $\theta$.

$V_{\rm lin}(\theta)$ is a weighted cosine average between the major and minor eigenvalues — it is maximal when selection aligns with the long axis and minimal when it aligns with the short axis. The curvature term $V_{\rm quad}$ does not care about gradient direction at all — it only depends on how curved the landscape is and how broad the cloud is.

$V_{\rm lin} = \mathbf{b}^\top\mathbf{G}\mathbf{b}$ with $\mathbf{G}$ diagonal: this is simply $b_1^2\lambda_1 + b_2^2\lambda_2 = \|\mathbf{b}\|^2(\lambda_1\cos^2\theta + \lambda_2\sin^2\theta)$. It is the variance of the population projected onto the gradient direction.

$V_{\rm quad} = \tfrac{1}{2}\text{tr}[(\mathbf{H}\mathbf{G})^2] = \tfrac{1}{2}\gamma^2\text{tr}[\mathbf{G}^2] = \tfrac{1}{2}\gamma^2(\lambda_1^2+\lambda_2^2)$. No $\mathbf{b}$ enters.

With $\mathbf{H} = -\gamma\mathbf{I}$:

$$(\mathbf{H}\mathbf{G})^2 = \gamma^2\mathbf{G}^2, \quad \text{tr}[\gamma^2\mathbf{G}^2] = \gamma^2(\lambda_1^2+\lambda_2^2)$$

So $V_{\rm quad} = \tfrac{1}{2}\gamma^2(\lambda_1^2+\lambda_2^2)$, independent of $\theta$. The exit angle $\theta^*$ (where $\mathcal{A}_g = \alpha$) satisfies:

$$\cos^2\!\theta^* = \frac{\kappa_0(1-\alpha)/\alpha - \lambda_2}{\lambda_1-\lambda_2}, \quad \kappa_0 = \frac{V_{\rm quad}}{\|\mathbf{b}\|^2}$$

The channel is exit-proof under rotation when $\kappa_0 < (1-\alpha)/\alpha \cdot \lambda_2/\lambda_1$ — when even full misalignment keeps $\mathcal{A}_g$ above $\alpha$.

🔑

$V_{\rm quad}$ is blind to gradient direction. Rotation erodes the channel silently — no variance loss, no curvature change, no signal in the raw genetic statistics.

The only diagnostic is $r_{\rm env} \ll 0$: a strongly negative environmental contribution to $r(t)$ with positive or zero covariance contribution. This sign pattern does not arise in Act II (optimum approach) and is the fingerprint of Act III.
04

Distinguishing Act II from Act III

Both Act II and Act III produce falling $\mathcal{A}_g$. The rate diagnostic $r(t)$ separates them by their mechanistic signatures:

Act II (optimum approach): $r_{\rm env} \approx 0$, $r_{\rm cov} < 0$. The landscape has not changed — the population has moved into a less favourable region by approaching the peak. The genetic structure is eroding the channel from within. Intervention: find new selective targets.

Act III (gradient rotation): $r_{\rm env} \ll 0$, $r_{\rm cov} \geq 0$. The environment has rotated the landscape at the current position. The covariance may still be favourably structured for the old gradient — but the old gradient is gone. Intervention: introgress variance aligned with the new direction.

In the Darwin's finches data from Daphne Major:

The 1977–1983 period was Act II: the population tracked the drought-selected beak depth optimum, the lag narrowed, and $r_{\rm cov}$ turned negative as variance compressed along the now-irrelevant directional axis.

The 1983 El Niño was Act III: a flood year changed the seed community, rotating the gradient toward beak width. The covariance structure had not changed yet — it was still elongated along depth. But $r_{\rm env}$ turned strongly negative. Two acts, same falling $\mathcal{A}_g$, opposite interventions.

In Act II: $\dot{\mathbf{b}} = \mathbf{H}\mathbf{G}\mathbf{b}$ (motion along the landscape, no environmental term). This contributes to $r_{\rm motion}$ and $r_{\rm cov}$ but not $r_{\rm env}$.

In Act III: $\dot{\mathbf{b}} = \mathbf{b}_u\dot{u}$ (landscape reshaping by environment $u$). This drives $r_{\rm env} = 2(\mathbf{b}^\top\mathbf{G}\mathbf{b}_u/V_{\rm lin})\dot{u} - (\text{tr}[\mathbf{G}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{G}\mathbf{H}_u]/V_{\rm quad})\dot{u}$. The sign of $r_{\rm env}$ is determined by whether the environmental shift is rotating the gradient toward or away from $\mathbf{g}_{\max}$.
