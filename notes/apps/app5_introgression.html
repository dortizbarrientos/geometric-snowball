APP 05 / 05 · INTROGRESSION CRITERION

The Introgression
Criterion

"Does a wide cross open or close the additive channel — and can we predict which before making it?"

Prerequisite: Apps 1–4 Key result: $\delta\mathcal{A}_g \propto (\mathbf{b}^\top\mathbf{v})^2/V_{\rm lin} - \mathbf{v}^\top\mathbf{H}\mathbf{G}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{v}/V_{\rm quad}$
01

The practical question

In breeding and in evolution, new genetic material enters a population from outside — through a deliberate wide cross, a hybridisation event, or natural migration. The incoming variance is a pulse in the covariance matrix $\mathbf{G}$. The channel framework asks: does this pulse deepen the predictive regime, or erode it?

The answer depends entirely on the direction of incoming variance — not its magnitude alone.

Imagine the population's covariance cloud as an oval. The fitness gradient points in a specific direction. Adding variance from outside is like inflating the oval — but only in the direction the new material carries.

If the new variance inflates the oval along the gradient direction, $V_{\rm lin}$ rises more than $V_{\rm quad}$ and the channel deepens. If it inflates across the gradient (or into high-curvature directions), $V_{\rm quad}$ rises more and the channel erodes.

The incoming pulse is $\Delta\mathbf{G} = \sigma_\delta^2\,\mathbf{v}\mathbf{v}^\top$ — a rank-1 update in direction $\mathbf{v}$. The channel change is:

$$\delta V_{\rm lin} \approx \sigma_\delta^2\,(\mathbf{b}^\top\mathbf{v})^2, \quad \delta V_{\rm quad} \approx \sigma_\delta^2\,\mathbf{v}^\top\mathbf{H}\mathbf{G}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{v}$$

The ratio $\delta V_{\rm lin}/\delta V_{\rm quad}$ determines whether $\mathcal{A}_g$ rises or falls.

In pre-breeding, "wide crosses" use exotic germplasm — wild relatives, landraces, or varieties adapted to other environments — to introduce variance into elite breeding populations facing yield plateaus. The standard approach is to maximise variance contribution. But the channel criterion says: maximise gradient-aligned variance contribution while minimising curvature-exposed contribution. Direction matters more than magnitude.

In evolution, natural hybridisation between diverging populations can either increase or decrease local predictability. Whether introgression facilitates or constrains adaptive evolution at any given moment depends on the direction of incoming variance relative to the current selection gradient.

02

The rank-1 variance pulse

Model introgression as a rank-1 update to the covariance matrix: $\mathbf{G} \to \mathbf{G} + \sigma_\delta^2\,\mathbf{v}\mathbf{v}^\top$, where $\mathbf{v}$ is the direction of incoming variance and $\sigma_\delta^2$ is its magnitude.

INTROGRESSION PULSE MODEL · Paper SI Appendix S8
$$\mathbf{G}_{\rm new} = \mathbf{G} + \underbrace{\sigma_\delta^2\,\mathbf{v}\mathbf{v}^\top}_{\Delta\mathbf{G}}$$
A rank-1 update is the minimal model: variance enters along a single direction $\mathbf{v}$ (the direction of trait divergence in the donor population). Higher-rank updates are sums of rank-1 pulses and satisfy the same criterion componentwise.

The key question is not "how much variance does introgression add?" but "in which direction?" Two crosses adding the same total variance can have opposite effects on channel depth — one deepening it, one eroding it — depending on whether the incoming direction $\mathbf{v}$ is closer to the gradient $\mathbf{b}$ or to the high-curvature directions of $\mathbf{H}$.

Expand the variance components to first order in $\sigma_\delta^2$:

$$V_{\rm lin}^{\rm new} = (\mathbf{b}^\top\!+\sigma_\delta^2\mathbf{0})(\mathbf{G}+\sigma_\delta^2\mathbf{v}\mathbf{v}^\top)\mathbf{b} = V_{\rm lin} + \sigma_\delta^2\,(\mathbf{b}^\top\mathbf{v})^2 + O(\sigma_\delta^4)$$ $$V_{\rm quad}^{\rm new} = \tfrac{1}{2}\text{tr}[(\mathbf{H}(\mathbf{G}+\Delta\mathbf{G}))^2] = V_{\rm quad} + \sigma_\delta^2\,\mathbf{v}^\top\mathbf{H}\mathbf{G}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{v} + O(\sigma_\delta^4)$$

The Mathematica notebook confirms these via Series[VlinPlus - VlinQ, {sigD, 0, 2}] and similarly for $V_{\rm quad}$.

03

The criterion

Introgression deepens the channel ($\delta\mathcal{A}_g > 0$) when the benefit to $V_{\rm lin}$ outweighs the cost to $V_{\rm quad}$.

INTROGRESSION CRITERION · Paper Discussion
$$\delta\mathcal{A}_g > 0 \quad \Longleftrightarrow \quad \underbrace{\frac{(\mathbf{b}^\top\mathbf{v})^2}{V_{\rm lin}}}_{\text{gradient benefit}} > \underbrace{\frac{\mathbf{v}^\top\mathbf{H}\mathbf{G}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{v}}{V_{\rm quad}}}_{\text{curvature cost}}$$
The criterion is a signed budget: benefit when incoming variance is gradient-aligned, cost when it amplifies curvature exposure. $\mathbf{v}$ is controllable by germplasm choice — this is a practical guide for pre-breeding.

The benefit term $(\mathbf{b}^\top\mathbf{v})^2/V_{\rm lin}$: how well does the incoming direction $\mathbf{v}$ align with the gradient $\mathbf{b}$? If $\mathbf{v}$ is parallel to $\mathbf{b}$, the benefit is large. If orthogonal, the benefit is zero.

The cost term $\mathbf{v}^\top\mathbf{H}\mathbf{G}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{v}/V_{\rm quad}$: how much does the incoming direction amplify curvature exposure? Curvature-aligned directions carry high cost.

Optimal introgression: choose $\mathbf{v}$ to maximise benefit/cost. The optimal direction is the leading eigenvector of $\mathbf{G}^{-1/2}\mathbf{b}\mathbf{b}^\top\mathbf{G}^{-1/2}$ minus a curvature penalty.

Geometrically, the criterion compares two projections: (1) the projection of $\mathbf{v}$ onto the gradient direction (benefit); and (2) the projection of $\mathbf{v}$ through the curvature operator $\mathbf{H}\mathbf{G}\mathbf{H}$ (cost). When the gradient and the curvature axes are orthogonal (as in an isotropic Hessian away from the major axis), benefit and cost are separable and the criterion simplifies.

For breeding: a wide cross from a donor population locally adapted to a different environment will introduce variance in the direction of its own selection history. If that direction overlaps with the current breeding gradient, it helps. If it introduces variance orthogonal to the gradient (e.g., adaptation to drought when the breeding programme targets yield under ideal conditions), it adds curvature load without directional benefit.

The criterion gives a single computable number for each candidate donor: positive = helps, negative = harms. This converts germplasm bank characterisation into channel-aware pre-breeding decisions.

From first-order expansions of $V_{\rm lin}$ and $V_{\rm quad}$:

$$\delta\mathcal{A}_g \approx \frac{\delta V_{\rm lin}\cdot V_{\rm quad} - \delta V_{\rm quad}\cdot V_{\rm lin}}{(V_{\rm lin}+V_{\rm quad})^2}\cdot\sigma_\delta^2$$

The sign of $\delta\mathcal{A}_g$ is determined by $\delta V_{\rm lin}/V_{\rm lin} - \delta V_{\rm quad}/V_{\rm quad} > 0$, which gives the criterion above. Note the structural parallel to $r(t) = \dot{V}_{\rm lin}/V_{\rm lin} - \dot{V}_{\rm quad}/V_{\rm quad}$: introgression is a single-generation version of the covariance route of $r$.

🔑

The introgression criterion is the static counterpart of $r_{\rm cov}$: a one-generation version of the covariance route of $r(t)$.

Beneficial introgression increases $r_{\rm cov}$ for that generation — it contributes positively to the rate of channel deepening. Harmful introgression decreases $r_{\rm cov}$. The same framework that diagnoses channels over evolutionary time also evaluates individual germplasm contributions in a breeding programme.
04

Two canonical cases

$\mathbf{v} \parallel \mathbf{b}$ (introgression perfectly aligned with gradient):

$\delta V_{\rm lin} = \sigma_\delta^2 \|\mathbf{b}\|^2 \cdot \|\mathbf{v}\|^2$ (maximal benefit). The curvature cost $\delta V_{\rm quad} = \sigma_\delta^2 \mathbf{v}^\top\mathbf{H}\mathbf{G}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{v}$ is nonzero but smaller. $\delta\mathcal{A}_g > 0$: the cross opens the channel.

$\mathbf{v} \perp \mathbf{b}$ (introgression orthogonal to gradient):

$\delta V_{\rm lin} = 0$ (zero benefit — the projection $\mathbf{b}^\top\mathbf{v} = 0$). The curvature cost $\delta V_{\rm quad} > 0$ still adds noise to the quadratic term. $\delta\mathcal{A}_g < 0$: the cross harms the channel even though it adds total genetic variance.

This is why raw variance maximisation is an incomplete criterion for germplasm choice — variance orthogonal to the gradient is pure curvature load.

Beneficial introgression in Darwin's finches: hybridisation from G. scandens into G. fortis in the 1970s introduced variance in beak length — a direction aligned with the post-drought selection gradient. $\mathcal{A}_g$ increased.

Potentially harmful introgression in cereals: wide crosses from drought-adapted wild relatives introduce variance in root architecture and water-use efficiency — directions often orthogonal to the yield gradient in irrigated breeding environments. Total variance rises, but $\mathcal{A}_g$ may fall, making genomic models less reliable before the new variance is directionally selected.
