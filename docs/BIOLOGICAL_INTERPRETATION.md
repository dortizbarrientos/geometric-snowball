# Biological interpretation and failure modes

## What a channel means

A channel is not simply the direction containing the most genetic variance. It is a direction in which available additive variation and local fitness curvature jointly produce a strong return toward the optimum. In the isotropic-fitness limit it reduces to \(\mathbf g_{\max}\); under anisotropic selection it can rotate substantially away from \(\mathbf g_{\max}\).

The eigenvalues of \(\mathbf S\) are local response rates. The leading eigenspace identifies fast modes. A spectral gap measures how well separated those modes are from alternatives.

## What rotation means

Channel rotation means that two populations have become efficient at responding along different multivariate combinations. It does not by itself imply that hybrids are unfit. The parental covariance structures must also place hybrid variance in trait combinations that are costly under the parental fitness curvatures, or recombination must add segregation variance in costly directions.

## Why the snowball is early

Under smooth divergent selection, channel angles change linearly to first order. Any squared subspace discrepancy therefore begins quadratically. This mechanism can operate before many lineage-specific substitutions have fixed and can be generated by sorting standing variation.

The early exponent is not universal. Drift-like channel diffusion produces a linear expectation for squared displacement. Abrupt environmental shifts, eigenvalue crossings, changing channel dimension, or strongly nonlinear dynamics can alter the local law.

## Relationship to ecological reproductive isolation

The most direct early effect is expected to be extrinsic and habitat dependent. Hybrid means may fall between parental optima, and hybrid covariance may extend into steeply penalised directions. The framework predicts both components separately.

F1 hybrids can show heterosis because dominance and masking of deleterious recessives are outside the additive covariance argument. The geometric covariance mechanism should often be clearer in F2 or advanced recombinants, where segregation exposes new combinations and covariance structures.

## Relationship to DMIs

The framework does not redescribe DMIs as angles. Instead, it proposes an ecological precursor. If curvature-weighted hybrid load lowers effective migration, lineage-specific substitutions can establish more readily. Once different substitutions accumulate, standard epistatic mechanisms can generate intrinsic incompatibilities.

This produces a possible ratchet: an early, potentially reversible ecological barrier changes the population-genetic conditions under which later, less reversible intrinsic barriers evolve.

## Conditions that can falsify or reverse the mechanism

The mechanism is weakened or rejected when:

- channels rotate but curvature-weighted hybrid load is absent;
- the trace term has the opposite sign and hybrids show covariance-based heterosis;
- raw genetic distance predicts hybrid fitness as well as the full \(\mathbf G\)-by-\(\mathbf H\) model;
- channel divergence is predominantly stochastic, nonrepeatable, or linear in squared distance under replicated directional selection;
- population pairs with greater predicted susceptibility do not rotate more rapidly under controlled perturbation;
- reduced ecological gene flow does not increase independent substitution or intrinsic isolation in mechanistic population-genetic simulations; or
- inferred channels are not robust to posterior uncertainty, trait choice, regularisation, or the shared metric.

These are useful outcomes. The theory is valuable only insofar as it separates conditions producing breakdown, no effect, and heterosis.
